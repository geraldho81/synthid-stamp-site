Google DeepMind watermarks Gemini text

SynthID-Text is Google DeepMind's generative text watermark. Google DeepMind leaves model training unchanged and changes only the sampling procedure, the step where the model picks the next word. You do not need the underlying LLM to detect the mark.

Do this: Ask the generating service whether it applied the mark.

How it works

SynthID-Text uses tournament sampling. Google DeepMind's experiments generally use 30 layers unless otherwise stated.

The production configuration is non-distortionary. It uses two competitors per match, plus repeated context masking. A distortionary configuration stamps a stronger mark and costs quality.

SynthID-Text integrates with speculative sampling for production speed. On Gemma 7B-IT running on 4 v5e TPUs, tournament sampling increased latency by 0.57%.

Do this: If you run an LLM service, apply the mark at sampling time. You do not retrain the model.

The 20 million test

Google DeepMind analysed approximately 20 million watermarked and unwatermarked Gemini responses. Thumbs-up rates differed by 0.01%, with watermarked replies higher. Thumbs-down rates differed by 0.02%, with watermarked replies lower. Both differences were statistically insignificant and inside the 95% confidence intervals.

Google DeepMind then productionised SynthID-Text in Gemini and Gemini Advanced.

Human raters compared Gemma 7B-IT on 3,000 ELI5 prompts across five aspects. Raters showed no significant preference.

Do this: If someone claims a watermark wrecks writing quality, cite this live Gemini test.

The catch

The generating service has to apply the mark. Open-source models and other labs are not covered.

Stealing, spoofing, scrubbing, and paraphrasing weaken the mark.

SynthID-Text is complementary to post-hoc methods.

Do this: Check whether the generating service applied the mark. Assume stealing, spoofing, scrubbing, and paraphrasing can weaken it. Use post-hoc methods alongside this check.
